How to buy BTC (Bitcoin)

In the US

  • Coinbase / Coinbase Advanced (BTC-USD)
  • Kraken (BTC/USD)
  • Uphold (BTC)

Outside the US

  • A supported exchange (BTC/USDT spot and perpetual futures)
  • Kraken in many countries
  1. Pick an exchange that lists BTC where you live. Compare fees and the trading pair before you sign up.
  2. Create an account and verify your identity. Regulated exchanges ask for ID and proof of address before you can deposit.
  3. Fund it. A bank transfer (ACH/SEPA) is usually the cheapest; cards cost more.
  4. Buy BTC on the spot market (BTC-USD). A limit order at a level you chose (see the support levels above) avoids paying the spread on a market order.
  5. Decide where to keep it. For long-term holdings, consider moving it to your own wallet (below). When you withdraw, pick the Bitcoin network your wallet supports: sending on the wrong network can lose the funds.

Self-custody: keeping BTC in your own wallet

Keep long-term holdings in a hardware wallet like Ledger. Coins on an exchange are an IOU from that exchange. A hardware wallet keeps the private keys on a small offline device, so you approve every transfer yourself. Before you send BTC to any wallet, check that it supports the Bitcoin network, write the recovery phrase on paper (never in a photo or a cloud note), and send a small test amount first.

BTC FAQ

Where can I buy BTC in the US?

BTC is listed for US customers on Coinbase / Coinbase Advanced (BTC-USD), Kraken (BTC/USD), Uphold (BTC). Availability varies by state (for example, Uphold doesn't serve New York), so check the exchange's own list before signing up.

Where can I buy BTC outside the US?

Outside the US, a supported exchange (BTC/USDT spot and perpetual futures) and Kraken in many countries list it. Availability depends on your country; check the exchange's supported-regions page.
